# Management skill: refinements from the record (2026-11-14)

Rule lines worth adding to the management skill, deduplicated across the 46 subjects in learnings.md and grouped under the skill's own sections. Each line has a check, and each names the subjects that justify it. Anything the skill already says (the seven-day review-age signal, created versus closed, the per-owner cap, the MVP slice named before work, no worker or branch on Later, overrides recorded) is left out.

## 1. The four lanes

- **R1. The lane comes from the store, not the profile.** A profile that says parked, dormant or pending while its project has tickets in review, in progress or newly ready is stale. Check: `drew validate` warns on every profile whose status contradicts its ticket statuses. Subjects: lightinglab, plantriloquist, appforge, nfl-analytics.
- **R2. Parked means frozen.** A parked project gains no new ready or in-progress tickets; its review items older than seven days go to the owner once as approve-or-close; a dormant project keeps only its repo-or-archive decision open, and its review tickets move to Parked with a dated reason. Check: the weekly review lists every parked project whose ready, in-progress or review count rose since it was parked. Subjects: plantriloquist, nfl-analytics.
- **R3. Parked carries a testable restart trigger.** The trigger is a dated field in the profile (for example "revenue at the main business" or "a paying week"); when the pass finds it met, the project moves to Later. Check: every parked profile has a trigger field and the pass prints whether it is met. Subjects: philanthropy, quorum.
- **R4. An undescribed project holds one question.** A project whose status is pending, or whose "what is this" or identity question is open, carries only that question and an adopt-or-archive decision; every other ticket is a child of the decision, in Later, and nothing is ready until the owner states the outcome in one paragraph. Check: no ready ticket exists in a project with pending status. Subjects: showcase, ganado, become-a-data-analyst.
- **R5. Parked as a product is not parked as a method.** When a profile parks the product but keeps the method (cases, rules, tests) or a collaborator workflow alive, one named enabling slice is allowed and counted apart from product work; any ready feature ticket that is not a case, rule, test or that enabling slice is flagged. Check: `drew validate` flags product features in a project parked as a product. Subjects: appforge, temujin.
- **R6. Later has an exit, not just a record.** A Later project with no ticket movement for 14 days is reviewed once in the weekly review and either promoted to Planned with its MVP ticket or parked with a dated reason; a project in exploring status carries a kill-or-continue test (a count of replies, reviews or sign-ups by a date). Check: the weekly review lists Later projects idle 14 days and exploring projects without a test. Subjects: prompting, writing, channel, science-stats.
- **R7. Commercial plans wait for the core.** Pricing, checkout, sign-up forms, mockups and "200%" polish for a product whose core check, precision gate or first paying user has not shipped go to Later, however ready they look. Check: Desk flags any ticket with a payment, sign-up or polish outcome whose depends_on chain holds an unbuilt core ticket, or whose project has zero clients. Subjects: Backseat Analyst beta plan, backseat-analyst, tutoring, algo.
- **R8. Cleanup is Planned, never p0.** A cleanup, merge or dedupe task is Planned unless a section 2 signal makes it Now; its MVP is one batch with a before and after count, and it reports net open tickets against tickets created during the pass. Check: no cleanup ticket is p0 without a named signal; the pass record shows net open alongside created. Subjects: store cleanup, Pass 2.
- **R9. Rules and skills spend a hidden budget.** Each new promoted rule or skill counts against the Planned budget (at most N per week, each naming the failure it prevents), and the context it costs per turn is reported. Check: the weekly review shows skills promoted this week and the per-turn token cost of all skills. Subjects: standards, Pass 3.

## 2. The signals that move the balance

- **R10. Creation is capped where closing is slow.** When a project's ready plus review exceeds a set multiple of its seven-day closes, or its ready count exceeds its cap, new feature tickets in it land in Later and only the MVP slice is promoted; coworker rows land as proposals in Later, not as ready tickets. Check: `drew stats` prints the ratio per project and `drew validate` refuses a new ready feature in a capped project. Subjects: desk, algo, backseat-analyst, funding, jre, Pass 3.
- **R11. p0 stays rare.** At most five open p0 per project and p0 under a set percent of all open tickets; a sixth forces a dated demotion of one; p0 plus p1 above about 35 percent of a project's open tickets is flagged as priority inflation. Check: `drew stats` prints p0 count and the p0-plus-p1 share per project. Subjects: desk, management, consulting, jre, algo.
- **R12. Review debt is Now.** A review queue over 25 items in one project, or over a project's cap, pulls that project into Now: no new ready ticket there gets a worker until the queue is cleared, and the first action is approve or reject by lane rule, not deduplication. Check: `drew stats` lists review count and oldest age per project and owner. Subjects: jeby-us, consulting, musicalgames, learninglabs, Pass 2, store cleanup.
- **R13. Built but not live is Now.** A slice counts as shipped only when it runs on the instance people use (the live page, the installed tool); a p0 that is built but not installed or merged stays Now with the install or merge step in blocked_on, and the pass reports "built but not live" per parent ticket. Check: every review ticket with a merged or built change names its live check and its result. Subjects: identity work, eighteen-point review, real-estate launch, jeby-us.
- **R14. The owner's one-minute steps are dated and lead.** Every ticket waiting on the owner's credentials, body or click carries a dated blocked_on and the one-minute step; the oldest is shown in the signals, and a step that gates a build which already passed QC is an overdue ask at once and leads the pass report. Check: no owner-bound ticket has an empty or undated blocked_on. Subjects: security, JRE sequencing, identity work, fightplan.
- **R15. A stall ratio flags projects that only grow.** A project with more than 80 percent of its tickets in ready or review and under 5 percent done is flagged. Check: `drew stats` prints the ratio and the weekly review lists flagged projects. Subjects: management, backseat-analyst, funding, brand, partner.
- **R16. Decisions closed are not progress on their own.** For an income-first or revenue project, the pass names the single next real-world action and the shortest chain from its p0 to first revenue, and counts decisions closed against actions closed; closed decisions with no closed action is a stall signal. Check: the pass report prints both counts for every income-first project. Subjects: fightplan, tutoring, consulting.
- **R17. A pile of owner choices becomes one sitting.** When more than half of a project's open tickets are owner decisions and none is done, the pass offers one batched decision session; any decision with a default is taken under executive-decisions instead of waiting. Check: the pass report lists projects over the half-decisions line. Subjects: brand, fightplan, consulting.
- **R18. A repeat is fixed by a verified route on the first repeat.** On the second ask, the ticket's smallest_step is executed with a route the agent has tested end to end with its own tools, and every step written for the owner is checked against the vendor's screen or docs first; a Now item with more than three subtickets closes or retires its spillover before adding more. Check: the repeat ticket's Evidence shows the agent's own test before the owner is told; subticket count is shown. Subjects: shared-drive access saga, eighteen-point review.
- **R19. Deadlines and fixed dates get their own signals.** A review gate unclaimed past its deadline is a Now signal; a fixed-date project carries backward checkpoint tickets (the date minus 6, 4 and 2 weeks) and is flagged in any week where no ticket moved. Check: the weekly review lists overdue gates and fixed-date projects with no movement. Subjects: partner package, cosmology.

## 3. The MVP rule

- **R20. The MVP is a tag, not a sentence.** One ticket per project carries a literal `mvp` tag and is ranked first in its ready list; every project in Planned or Later with ready tickets has one; a Later project's MVP lists its money and legal gates as blockers; a new idea's thesis ticket names it in its first line. Check: `drew validate` warns on any project with ready tickets and no `mvp` tag. Subjects: desk, writing, driver-tracking, channel, quorum, florida-man, learninglabs, cosmology.
- **R21. A decision, research item or first task is not the slice.** The MVP is something a real user can open: a staging URL, a live page, a file sent, a run on one real input. Check: an `mvp` ticket whose type is decision or research, or whose outcome names no thing a user opens, is refused. Subjects: real-estate launch, eighteen-point review, management.
- **R22. Nothing new is promoted while the MVP is open.** Until the `mvp` ticket reaches review, new feature tickets in that project stay in Later, the ready count is capped at the MVP rows plus five, and rows beyond the MVP (paid edition, "200%" look and motion, later platforms) are Later children of the MVP ticket, never peer ready tickets; an intake that splits into more than about 20 tickets follows the same shape. Check: `drew validate` flags a ready feature that is not under the MVP while the MVP is open. Subjects: jre, backseat-analyst, algo, route-optimizer, funding, learninglabs.
- **R23. The MVP serves priority one.** When the owner states a priority order, the MVP slice serves priority 1 only, and rows for lower priorities are marked "after MVP ships". Check: the `mvp` ticket names the priority it serves. Subjects: territory, florida-man.
- **R24. Clearance comes before build, and the slice reaches a reviewer first.** For any launch, data, licence, advertising and name clearance tickets come before the build tickets in the dependency chain; for deadline-bound work, the named smallest slice goes to the first reviewer before the full package is built. Check: the launch chain's first ticket is a clearance check; a deadline ticket shows the slice's delivery date before the package's. Subjects: real-estate launch, partner package.
- **R25. A Now item's release is its first slice.** When a Now item exists (a stale live site, a repeated ask), the first slice of any related parent is the release or merge, and Planned subtickets do not start building until it is merged or its blocked_on names who acts. Check: the parent's first child by order is the release ticket. Subjects: eighteen-point review, jeby-us.

## 4. Each pass and each week

- **R26. A pass opens with an exit line and a cap.** Each pass writes one measurable exit line and its MVP slice before work starts (for example "the sites live, created minus closed at or below zero"), may not end with created minus closed above its stated cap, and an open-ended improvement requirement still names a cap. Check: the pass record's first section holds the exit line and the cap, and its last section reports against both. Subjects: the three passes, Pass 2, Pass 3.

## 5. Where it lives

- **R27. Management ships its own dashboard first.** The management project's Now lane's first row is the one-page dashboard (created versus closed, review age, p0 count, the stall and inflation flags), named as its `mvp`, ahead of every other weekly-review task. Check: the weekly review ticket renders those numbers from `drew stats`, not typed. Subjects: management, the three passes.
- **R28. The new thresholds are data.** `desk.toml` `[management]` gains: the creation multiple, the p0 cap per project and as a percent, the review cap per project, the MVP overflow (five), the Later idle days (14), the stall ratio (80 and 5 percent), and the skills-per-week budget. Check: `drew stats` reads each from `desk.toml` and prints the value it used. Subjects: desk, consulting, backseat-analyst, prompting, management, standards.
- **R29. Every project carries its lane tag.** The profile and its tickets carry `lane:now|planned|later|parked` explicitly, matching R1. Check: `drew validate` warns on a profile without a lane tag. Subjects: channel, funding, lightinglab.
